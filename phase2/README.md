## Challenge Setting

- **Data:** 10 face clips of MEAD identity `M003` (113 frames each, 640×480).
- **Budget:** `L∞ ≤ 16/255` per pixel.
- **Threat models:** *reference-based* (the attacker samples one frame from a protected clip and
  feeds it, together with a text prompt, to an image-to-video model) and *tuning-based* (the
  attacker fine-tunes a video diffusion model with LoRA on all protected clips, then generates new
  videos from text prompts).
- **Known model:** [Wan2.2-TI2V-5B](https://huggingface.co/Wan-AI/Wan2.2-TI2V-5B-Diffusers), with
  white-box access.
- **Tracks (for both threat models):** *Effectiveness* (known model), *Robustness* (after hidden
  temporal attacks) and *Transferability* (hidden generation models). The prompts, the
  reference-frame sampling strategy, the scoring models, the temporal attacks and the transfer
  models are hidden.
- **Score:** invisibility plus the harmonic mean of the ten track scores; see
  [`eval_competition/README.md`](eval_competition/README.md) for the metric definitions.

## Method

A two-layer framework, optimized face first, background second, and combined with per-frame face
masks under the shared `L∞ ≤ 16/255` budget:

- **Prior-Aligned Face Protection.** A structure-aligned perturbation built from a synthetic target
  identity (`assets/original.png`) is placed on the face and follows it across frames through
  facial landmarks.
- **White-Box Background Protection.** The Phase 1 attack on Wan2.2, restricted to the non-face
  region: **V-capacity collapse** and **K-attract** on self-attention, plus VAE latent disruption.

All hyper-parameters are hard-coded at the top of `protect/methods/my_method.py`.

## Target Identity Image — Generation and Reproducibility

The target identity image utilized in our original submission was synthesized through iterative,
multi-turn conversational interactions with ChatGPT (ChatGPT Image Aug 4, 2026). Given the stochastic nature of
generative models during multi-turn prompting across extended contexts, directly executing a
deterministic re-generation of the exact instance poses inherent sampling variation.

To boost the reproducibility of our approach, we performed supplementary validation experiments. Specifically, we observed that directly providing the target face identity image (provided as `protect/methods/assets/original.png`) to the model alongside a single, standardized prompt can produce synthetic faces with similar efficacy.

**The prompt is specified as follows:**

> Keep the exact pose, framing, white T-shirt, lighting, and green fabric background from the
> reference image, but replace the face with a completely different male identity. Make the new face
> similarly long and wide, with a larger prominent nose, larger bright blue eyes, lighter thinner
> eyebrows, and a clearly different jawline and chin shape. Give him short dark brown hair, slightly
> protruding ears, visible under-eye lines, subtle forehead wrinkles, and light facial hair with a
> sparse mustache and a short goatee, and clear, striking bright blue eyes. The expression should be
> a restrained, slightly awkward half-smile. His facial features seemed somewhat disproportionate, a
> bit like an old alien. Do not preserve or blend the reference identity. The face must read as a
> different person.

We provide 5 images generated by this prompt. The corresponding image assets are located at `protect/methods/assets/t_*.png`. 

We deployed these additionally generated 5 images to protect 10 video clips respectively, and evaluated the identity similarity on protected videos using the official ISM evaluation pipeline provided in the starting kit. The detailed ISM scores (averaged across the 10 clips for each image) are summarized below:

| Target image                                  | Mean ISM (10 clips) | Δ vs. original submission |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------- |
| `original.png` *(original submission)*        | 0.4742              | —                         |
| `t_1.png`                                     | 0.4922              | +0.0181                   |
| `t_2.png`                                     | 0.4783              | +0.0041                   |
| `t_3.png`                                     | 0.4743              | +0.0001                   |
| `t_4.png`                                     | 0.4714              | −0.0028                   |
| `t_5.png`                                     | 0.4728              | −0.0014                   |

A lower ISM means stronger identity protection.

## Face Models

The face layer needs two InsightFace model packs — **`antelopev2`** (detection + 2D/3D
landmarks, for the target alignment and the per-frame tracking) and **`buffalo_l`** (for the face
mask). Both are downloaded automatically on first use into `~/.insightface/`:

```
~/.insightface/models/antelopev2/{scrfd_10g_bnkps,1k3d68,2d106det}.onnx
~/.insightface/models/buffalo_l/{det_10g,2d106det}.onnx
```

Set `ANTELOPEV2_ROOT` only if you keep `antelopev2` elsewhere; the method looks for it at
`$ANTELOPEV2_ROOT/models/antelopev2/`.

> ⚠️ The `antelopev2` archive may unpack into a nested folder
> (`~/.insightface/models/antelopev2/antelopev2/*.onnx`). InsightFace then finds no models and
> fails with `assert 'detection' in self.models`. If that happens, move the `.onnx` files up one
> level into `~/.insightface/models/antelopev2/`.

## Hardware

The background attack runs 200 optimization steps per clip with 3 EOT samples each. Every sample
first runs 5 denoising steps through the full Wan2.2 DiT without gradients, then back-propagates
only through the VAE encoder and the DiT blocks up to the sampled target block (0, 3 or 6):

- **GPU VRAM: ≥ 32 GB recommended** (observed peak ≈ 30 GB by `nvidia-smi`). Produced on NVIDIA
  H100 NVL cards; an A100-40GB / H100 is sufficient, a 24 GB card is likely to OOM.
- **Runtime: ≈ 9 minutes per clip on an idle H100** (≈ 17 min when the machine is loaded), so
  **1.5–3 hours for all 10 clips** on one GPU.
- The clips are independent — the global RNGs are re-seeded from the same fixed seed before each
  clip — so the run can be sharded across GPUs (one clip per GPU).

Select a GPU with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=<id>` if the machine has several.

## Tested Environment

Python 3.10 + conda-forge `ffmpeg 5.0.1`; every Python package is pinned in
**`requirements-protect.txt`**. Use **`diffusers==0.37.1`**; other releases change the Wan2.2
pipeline / attention internals. The remaining pins are the versions we tested (torch 2.10.0+cu128,
transformers 4.57.6, kornia 0.8.2, insightface 0.7.3, onnxruntime-gpu 1.23.2, opencv-python
4.13.0.92, scikit-image 0.25.2, numpy 2.2.6, decord 0.6.0).

> **Note.** Bit-exact reproduction is not guaranteed. The random downsample-upsample EOT
> augmentation uses `area` and `bicubic` interpolation, whose CUDA backward passes have no
> deterministic implementation, so a rerun can end at a slightly different perturbation.
